Yes — night crashes are far more dangerous, though daytime sees more crashes overall. Among 2.27M NYPD collision records (2012–2026), the rush-hour afternoon peaks in crash volume (e.g., 161,000 crashes at 4 pm), but each night crash is much likelier to maim or kill: crashes between 7 pm and 6 am cause 0.39 injuries per crash versus 0.30 during the day, and their fatality rate is 28 deaths per 10,000 crashes — nearly triple the daytime rate of 10. The danger peaks in the pre-dawn hours: at 4 am, roughly 6.4 of every 1,000 crashes are fatal, about eight times the midday rate, when lighter traffic lulls drivers into higher speeds — the deadliest hour on NYC streets.
