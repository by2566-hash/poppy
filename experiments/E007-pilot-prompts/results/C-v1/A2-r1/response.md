ANSWER: Yes — NYC streets are far deadlier after dark, though less busy. Among 2.27M police-reported crashes (2012–mid-2026), crashes between 10pm and 6am kill 3.5 people per 1,000 crashes vs 1.2 in daytime — about 2.9× as deadly (0.33% vs 0.12% of crashes are fatal; 0.40 vs 0.32 injuries per crash). Night sees only 17% of crashes but 37% of deaths, with lethality peaking at 4am (6.4 deaths per 1,000 crashes, ~7× the midday rate). Most crashes and deaths still occur in daytime simply because there is far more traffic; per crash, a nighttime trip is roughly three times as likely to end in a death, a gap that holds in every recent year. (Severity per crash is used because traffic-exposure data is unavailable; slight under-reporting of minor nighttime crashes could inflate the ratio somewhat, but death counts are hard numbers.)
