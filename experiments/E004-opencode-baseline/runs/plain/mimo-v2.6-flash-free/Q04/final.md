**What the data shows**
- Sharp break at COVID: April 2020 logged 4,129 crashes vs 13,704 in Feb 2020 (−70%).
- No rebound since: latest 12 full months (Jul 2024–Jun 2025) average 7,275/mo vs 18,724 pre-COVID (−61%). 2026 YTD (Jan 1–Jun 11) is 36,424 vs 36,774 a year earlier (−1.0%) — the decline has flattened, not reversed.
- Injuries fell only −14%, and deaths did not fall at all (+9%, peaking at 297 in 2021). Motorist deaths rose from ~96/yr (2017–19) to ~108/yr (2022–25); pedestrian deaths were flat.
- Severity mix shifted hard: injuries per reported crash went 0.27 (2017–19) → 0.58 (2024), i.e. crashes became about twice as severe on average.
- The decline had already started pre-COVID: 2019 was −9% vs 2018 with injuries flat.

**Caveats**
- These are *police-reported* crashes only (MV-104AN: injury, death, or ≥$1,000 damage). The doubling of injuries-per-crash suggests fewer minor/low-damage collisions are ending up in the file — so the −58% likely overstates the true drop in collisions.
- No traffic-exposure (miles driven) field here, so I can't separate "fewer cars on the road" from "safer roads." Flat death totals alongside halved crash counts is not evidence of improved safety.
- 2012 (starts Jul) and 2026 (ends Jun 11) are partial years and excluded from averages; data is preliminary.
