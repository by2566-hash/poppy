**Answer: Driver inattention/distraction is the main cause — by a wide margin.**

Data: 2,269,187 crashes (2012-07-01 → 2026-06-11). 34% of rows record the primary factor as "Unspecified" (or blank), so shares below are of the **1,504,342 crashes with a recorded factor**.

| Rank | Primary contributing factor | Crashes | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Driver Inattention/Distraction** | 462,105 | **30.7%** |
| 2 | Failure to Yield Right-of-Way | 136,046 | 9.0% |
| 3 | Following Too Closely | 121,711 | 8.1% |
| 4 | Backing Unsafely | 82,577 | 5.5% |
| 5 | Other Vehicular | 71,429 | 4.7% |

- **3.4× more than the #2 cause.** Counting the whole distraction family (outside-car, passenger, electronic device, texting) → 486,419 crashes, 32.3%.
- Clustered "bad driving" causes (inattention, tailgating, improper passing/lane changes, failure to yield) dominate; impairment is comparatively rare (Alcohol 1.8%, and drugs aren't in the top 15).
- **Fatal crashes differ:** distraction still leads (434 of 2,269 fatal crashes, 19.1%) but **Unsafe Speed is nearly tied** (414, 18.2%), then Failure to Yield (13.7%) and Traffic Control Disregarded (12.3%). Speed kills far more than its 2.4% share of all crashes suggests.
- **Trend:** the distraction share rose from ~10% (2012) to ~25% (2017 onward) — but "Unspecified" fell from 59% to ~24% over the same period, so part of the rise is better reporting, not just worse driving.
