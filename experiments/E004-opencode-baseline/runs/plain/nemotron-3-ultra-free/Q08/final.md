Based on the NYC collision data (2012-2026), **e-bikes are associated with a rapidly growing safety concern**, though the full picture is nuanced.

## Key Findings

### Explosive Growth in E-Bike Crashes
- **2019**: 67 crashes → **2023**: 2,554 crashes (**38× increase**)
- E-bike crashes now represent ~2.7% of all NYC crashes (up from near-zero pre-2020)
- In 2023, e-bikes accounted for **17% of cyclist injuries** and **50% of cyclist fatalities** despite being far fewer in number than regular bicycles

### Disproportionate Fatality Risk
| Metric (2023) | E-Bike | Regular Bicycle |
|--------------|--------|-----------------|
| Crashes | 2,554 | 4,438 |
| Cyclist Injuries | 789 | 3,737 |
| Cyclist Fatalities | **12** | **12** |

E-bikes had **equal cyclist fatalities with 57% fewer crashes** — suggesting higher severity per crash.

### Pedestrian Impact
- ~1,000 pedestrians injured and 9 killed in e-bike crashes (2012-2026)
- Manhattan has the highest pedestrian injuries from e-bikes (273) despite fewer total crashes than Brooklyn

### Contributing Factors
Top factors in e-bike crashes:
1. **Driver Inattention/Distraction** (3,593)
2. **Failure to Yield Right-of-Way** (1,424)
3. **Traffic Control Disregarded** (809)
4. **Passing/Lane Usage Improper** (620)

### Geographic & Temporal Patterns
- **Brooklyn** leads in e-bike crashes (3,769), followed by Queens (2,157) and Manhattan (2,036)
- Peak hours: **4-6 PM** (evening rush) for both e-bikes and regular bikes
- E-bike crashes follow similar daily patterns but at much lower absolute volumes

## Caveats
- Data reflects **police-reported** crashes only (injury or $1,000+ damage)
- E-bike adoption surged ~2020 (delivery apps, Citi Bike e-bikes) — exposure increased
- "E-Bike" classification may be inconsistent in reporting
- Regular bicycle crashes have **declined** since 2019 while e-bike crashes surged

**Bottom line**: E-bikes are not inherently "making streets more dangerous" in aggregate — total crashes are down — but they represent a **new and rapidly growing risk category** with disproportionately severe outcomes for riders and measurable pedestrian impact. The 50% fatality share in 2023 warrants targeted safety interventions.
