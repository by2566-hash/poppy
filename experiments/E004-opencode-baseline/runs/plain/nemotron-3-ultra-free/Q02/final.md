**Answer: Cyclists are NOT safer in absolute terms — injuries and fatalities have increased, and cyclists now represent a larger share of traffic casualties. However, without cycling volume data (miles traveled, number of riders), we can't determine if risk *per cyclist* has changed.**

**Key findings (2013–2025 full years):**

| Metric | 2013 | 2019 | 2025 | Change |
|--------|------|------|------|--------|
| Cyclist injured | 4,075 | 4,986 | 5,358 | **+31%** |
| Cyclist killed | 11 | 31 | 23 | **+109%** |
| Cyclist share of all injuries | 7.4% | 8.9% | 10.8% | **+46%** |
| Cyclist share of all fatalities | 3.7% | 7.8% | 10.0% | **+170%** |

**Important context:** Total reported crashes dropped ~55% after 2019 (211K → 85K), likely due to COVID and reporting changes. The *rate per 1,000 crashes* appears to triple (~20 → ~60), but this is an artifact of the shrinking denominator, not necessarily more danger per cyclist.

**Conclusion:** More cyclists are being hurt/killed in absolute numbers and as a share of traffic victims. Whether individual risk has risen depends on how much cycling has grown — which this dataset doesn't capture.
