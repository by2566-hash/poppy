ANSWER: In 2013–2025, reported crashes peaked at 4–4:59 p.m. (7.12% of records) and were least common at 3–3:59 a.m. (1.21%), but crash timing alone does not show nighttime risk per trip. Among crashes with complete outcome counts, injury or death was reported in 28.47% of nighttime crashes versus 23.41% of daytime crashes, and fatal crashes made up 0.29% versus 0.11%—a difference in severity among crashes, not proof of higher risk for street users at night. Nighttime crashes produced 40.71 people injured or killed per 100 crashes, compared with 30.98 during daytime, indicating a higher casualty burden per crash but not higher risk per trip.
