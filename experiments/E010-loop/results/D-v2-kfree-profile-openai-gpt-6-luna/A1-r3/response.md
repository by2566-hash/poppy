Brooklyn’s reported pedestrian injury counts are lower in 2025 than in 2013, but pedestrian deaths are higher. I’m checking three complementary count measures—injured pedestrians, pedestrian deaths, and crashes with any pedestrian casualty—using complete calendar years so the partial 2026 data don’t skew the comparison.
ANSWER:
Brooklyn recorded 3,641 pedestrian injuries in 2013 and 2,835 in 2025, down 22.1%, although injuries have climbed from 1,781 in 2020.
Pedestrian deaths increased from 30 in 2013 to 39 in 2025 (+30%), so this severe-outcome measure did not improve.
Crashes involving a pedestrian injury or death fell from 3,498 in 2013 to 2,766 in 2025 (20.9%), but have risen from 1,724 in 2020.
